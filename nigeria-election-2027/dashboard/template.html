    How this works

    • Data: state results from the 2015, 2019 and 2023 presidential elections (2023 counts reproduce INEC's national totals to within 0.1%), plus registered voters, religious mix, geopolitical zone and each governor's party.
    • Machine learning: a ridge regression predicts each candidate's vote in each state from ten features: their previous vote base, home state and zone, running mate's home, how well the ticket's faith matches the state, the governor's party and incumbency. It is checked by hiding one state at a time and predicting it.
    • Ensemble: the regression is averaged 50/50 with a "same as 2023" baseline, where Obi inherits the Labour + NNPP vote (Kwankwaso is his running mate) and Atiku inherits the PDP vote.
    • Simulation: 20,000 runs add national, zonal and state-level swings, an economic penalty on the incumbent, turnout noise, and a 25% chance Atiku is removed from the ballot. Each run applies the Section 134 rule and, if needed, a runoff.
    • Limits: the model learns from only two elections and there is no reliable scientific polling, so treat the numbers as a structured estimate, not a certainty. It does not model rigging, violence, low turnout in insecure areas, or last-minute alliances.

    Sources for 2027 facts: INEC's final candidate list and the January 2027 timetable; reporting by Channels TV, Premium Times, The Sun, Guardian Nigeria, ThisDay and ICIR on candidacies, the 5 Nov 2026 ruling on Atiku and governor defections. Public forecasts to compare against: a 50,000-run Monte Carlo model (Tinubu 79%), Kalshi (Tinubu ~72%), the Political Intelligence Group's state map, and the Earth Elects and OSRS models, which lean towards Obi in a runoff.
